# Persona Flow Review Methodology

## Purpose

The review connects four evidence layers:

```text
Persona evidence
→ User-story requirement
→ Figma design decision
→ Defensible reason or finding
```

The AI is an evidence-constrained reviewer. It is not a synthetic user and does not replace usability testing.

## Evidence rules

### Valid persona evidence

- Research-backed goals and desired outcomes
- Responsibilities and task ownership
- Information needed for decisions
- Documented concerns and failure conditions
- Relevant working behavior
- Contextual constraints

### Do not treat as independent persona evidence

- Proposed screens or controls
- “The product should…” statements
- Product promises written into the persona
- Decorative biography
- Demographics with no effect on the task
- Assumptions generated by the reviewer

### Valid story evidence

- User-facing acceptance criteria
- Preconditions and triggers
- Required information and actions
- Required feedback and states
- Success conditions
- Explicit exclusions
- Approved decisions and amendments

Technical criteria are relevant only when they affect visible behavior, timing, permission, data safety, or system feedback.

## Persona selection

Use the explicit story actor whenever available.

If it is missing, use:

1. Scenario mapping
2. Role and permissions
3. Task ownership
4. Persona goals

Use multiple personas only when each has a distinct role in the flow. Review their perspectives separately.

## Figma interpretation

- Match by story ID before title.
- Treat each top-level story section as one review unit.
- Record section and screen node IDs.
- Use connector or prototype relationships for order.
- Treat nested sections as states or branches only when their naming and connections support that interpretation.
- Do not infer interaction from appearance alone.
- Do not assume a component instance has the same content as its source component; inspect the visible instance.
- When a flow is incomplete, use Insufficient evidence.

## Review questions

### Coverage

- Can the persona reach the stated outcome?
- Is every important requirement represented?
- Are critical non-happy states represented?
- Does the flow include excluded behavior?
- Is information consistent from one step to the next?

### Step-level walkthrough

- Does the user have a reason to take the intended action?
- Is that action visible?
- Does its label connect to the desired result?
- Does the next state show progress or success?

These questions identify likely friction. They do not predict actual behavior.

## Finding boundaries

### User need

An evidence-backed problem or outcome that is independent of a particular interface.

### Story requirement

Agreed product behavior or a condition the story must satisfy.

### Design decision

A chosen screen, sequence, control, label, state, or presentation.

### UX friction

An evidence-backed risk that makes the agreed task harder to understand or complete.

### Requirement gap

Required behavior is absent or contradicted.

### Scope conflict

The design adds behavior with no approved source or conflicts with an explicit exclusion.

### Mapping drift

Figma and source documents refer to different stories, IDs, or current behavior.

### Accessibility concern

A potential barrier connected to an access need or WCAG. Route this to an accessibility or design-audit workflow. Do not claim conformance here.

### Evidence gap

The available material does not support a reliable conclusion.

## Assessment rubric

### Supports

Use when:

- The main objective has a complete path.
- Important user-facing requirements are represented.
- No core contradiction blocks the story.

Minor refinements may remain.

### Partially supports

Use when:

- The main path works.
- One or more important requirements, states, or decisions need correction.
- The objective remains achievable despite the gap.

### Needs attention

Use when:

- The flow contradicts a core requirement.
- The primary objective cannot be completed reliably.
- A safety, trust, or scope problem changes the intended solution.

### Insufficient evidence

Use when:

- The flow is incomplete or inaccessible.
- Sequence cannot be established.
- Required source documents are missing.
- Sources conflict without an approved precedence decision.

## Priority and compression

Rank findings by:

1. Objective blocked
2. Core requirement or scope conflict
3. Safety or trust risk
4. Meaningful task friction
5. Clarification

Keep a maximum of three observations per story. Do not create findings to fill the quota.

Each observation contains:

1. A short, plain-language title that states the problem or strength
2. **What we found:** what is visible or missing, described by name
3. **Why it matters:** the effect on the persona or the story goal
4. **Suggested fix:** one concrete change, omitted for strengths
5. A screen name in words, linked to the exact Figma node

Write every part in plain English (see `SKILL.md` section 9). Keep requirement IDs, node IDs, and layer names in links, not in sentences.

## Design rationale

Use this chain:

```text
Persona need
→ Story objective or requirement
→ Design decision
→ Reason it supports the user
```

Example:

```text
Riley needs to avoid contacting blocked identities
→ Story 4.2 requires suppression confirmation
→ The design shows excluded identities and reasons before sequence setup
→ Riley can verify the safety boundary before outreach is configured
```

## Fixing issues

A review finds issues. Fixing them is a separate, designer-driven step, one issue at a time.

### Which issues can be fixed

| Classification | Fix action | What a fix does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requirement gap | `figma-screen` or `figma-new-screen` | Adds or corrects the missing behavior, state, or screen |
| UX friction | `figma-screen` | Clarifies the label, order, emphasis, or feedback |
| Scope conflict | `figma-screen` | Removes or changes the unapproved behavior. The note card says this may be a product decision. |
| Mapping drift | `figma-rename` | Renames the Figma section to match the current story. Story files are never edited. |
| Accessibility concern | `audit-handoff` | Starts a separate accessibility review |
| Evidence gap or strength | `none` | Nothing to fix |

Use `figma-new-screen` only when the missing behavior needs its own screen or state, such as a missing error state.

### Principles

- **One issue at a time.** Each fix has its own chat, its own copy in Figma, and its own approval.
- **Safe copy first.** A fix is made on detached copies on the "Fixed issues" page. The Wireflows and Draft pages do not change until the designer approves.
- **The designer decides.** A designer instruction overrides the suggested fix. Conflicts with the story or scope are recorded, not blocked.
- **The source is updated, not the copy.** An approved fix is applied to the screen's main component on the Draft page. The Wireflows page then updates through its instances.
- **The assessment is earned.** After a fix is applied, the story is re-reviewed. Its assessment changes only when the evidence supports it.
- **Shared screens are flagged.** When the same component appears in other stories, those stories are marked for re-review, not silently re-assessed.

## Guardrails

- No first-person persona role-play
- No invented feelings or behavior
- No design preference without evidence
- No Figma edits in Review mode
- In Fix mode, no Figma edits outside the "Fixed issues" page
- No changes to the Draft or Wireflows pages without the designer's approval
- No WCAG compliance claim
- No scope claim without an approved scope source
- No flow-order inference from canvas coordinates alone
- No repeated project-wide finding under every story
- No jargon, codes, or shorthand that forces the reader to look something up
